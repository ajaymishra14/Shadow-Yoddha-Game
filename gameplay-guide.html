Shadow Yoddha: Gameplay Guide

A field guide to movement, hazards, combat and ability timing

The Eternal Run is built around a simple challenge: keep moving, read the path ahead and decide when to jump, strike or use a shadow ability. A successful run is not just about reacting quickly. It is about learning what each obstacle allows you to do and keeping a defensive option ready for the next encounter.

Read the obstacle before choosing an action

The game can place ordinary rocks, spike clusters and pillars in the runner's path. These hazards are not all handled in the same way. A jump is the basic response when there is room to clear an obstacle. A sword attack can remove ordinary obstacles that are close enough to the warrior. Shadow Power can clear several nearby ordinary obstacles at once, which makes it useful when the path becomes crowded.

Three special stone hazards are different: ancient rocks, titan rocks and cursed rocks are marked as indestructible in the game logic. A sword swing will bounce off them, and Shadow Power will not remove them. Treat these objects as movement challenges. Watch their height and position, then use a jump or Shadow Phase to pass safely.

Use the double jump deliberately

The warrior has two jumps before landing. The first jump gives the larger lift; the second jump can correct the route while airborne. Avoid spending both jumps immediately when a hazard is still far away. A late second jump can help clear a taller obstacle or adjust your landing. After touching the ground, the jump count resets.

Understand the two shadow abilities

Shadow Power

Shadow Power is a short burst that lasts about three quarters of a second and has a five-second cooldown. It removes ordinary obstacles within its nearby radius, but it does not destroy the three indestructible rock types. Use it to clear a cluster of breakable hazards rather than wasting it on a single special rock.

Shadow Phase

Shadow Phase lasts 5.5 seconds and has a 14-second cooldown. During the phase, the runner can pass through ordinary obstacles, and the phase can also protect the player from an enemy strike. Because its cooldown is longer, save it for a section where the route is difficult or an enemy's attack is about to land.

Combat: watch the warning, not just the weapon

Enemy encounters pause the normal obstacle flow. An enemy approaches, signals an incoming strike with a visible warning, attacks and then enters a recovery window. That recovery is the clearest opportunity to move in and use the sword. Jumping before the strike connects or activating Shadow Phase can avoid the hit. Standing still on the ground when the strike lands costs one of the warrior's three health points.

A successful sword hit reduces enemy health by one point. The sword needs the enemy to be within reach and at a compatible height; swinging too early or too far away will not deal damage. After a hit, a brief hit cooldown prevents repeated damage from one swing. Keep attacking during safe openings, but do not ignore the enemy's next warning.

Coins, score and saved progress

Coin groups appear above the ground. Each collected coin adds to the coin counter and contributes 25 points to the score. The score also rises while the run continues, with the rate increasing as speed builds. Defeating an encounter enemy adds a score bonus and coins. Your best run score is saved in local browser storage on the device you use; it is not a cloud account and may not follow you to another browser or device.

A simple practice routine

  1. Start a short run and practise one jump followed by a second jump before landing.
  2. Learn to identify the special rune-marked rocks and stop trying to destroy them.
  3. Use sword attacks on ordinary obstacles only when they are within range.
  4. During an encounter, wait for the warning cue and practise jumping over the strike.
  5. Use Shadow Phase as a defensive tool when a jump is not enough or a strike is imminent.
